# SKILL: Client Onboarding - INTELSOL

## PURPOSE
Provides a decision-making framework for determining the optimal email sequence structure for new B2B sales clients. Determines whether to create Position-based or Industry-based sequences and consolidates to a maximum of 2 sequences per client.

---

## DECISION FRAMEWORK

### STEP 1: Assess the Client's Value Proposition

**Ask yourself:**
1. **Does the client serve multiple industries with fundamentally different value propositions?**
   - If YES (e.g., Stojkov: Cosmetics + Food + Pharma) → Go to STEP 2A
   - If NO (e.g., Birografika: Labels for food/beverage only) → Go to STEP 2B

2. **Is the buyer journey fundamentally different by industry?**
   - Example: Cosmetics buyer cares about *appearance and brand positioning*
   - Example: Pharma buyer cares about *compliance and technical specs*
   - If YES → Consider Industry-based sequences
   - If NO → Position-based is better

3. **Or is the buyer journey fundamentally different by ROLE/POSITION?**
   - Example: Procurement Manager evaluates *cost, specs, capacity*
   - Example: CEO/Owner evaluates *partnership, reliability, economics*
   - If YES → Position-based is better

---

### STEP 2A: Multi-Industry Clients (e.g., Stojkov)

**If client serves multiple industries:**

**Check:** Are the compliance/regulatory/aesthetic requirements SO different that they need different messaging?
- Cosmetics = appearance, premium positioning, brand alignment
- Food = compliance, food-contact certifications, sustainability
- Pharma = regulated, specific certifications, technical specs

**If requirements differ by industry:**
- Consider creating Industry-based sequences (Cosmetics sequence, Food sequence, Pharma sequence)
- BUT: Cap at maximum 2 sequences to avoid complexity

**If requirements are similar across industries:**
- Use Position-based sequences (all industries follow the same procurement journey)
- **Default approach for Stojkov/Birografika:** Position-based (most effective)

---

### STEP 2B: Single-Industry or Niche Clients (e.g., Birografika)

**If client serves one primary industry or niche:**

**Ask:** Do different POSITIONS buy differently, or does everyone follow the same journey?

**If different positions buy differently:**
- Procurement Manager → Cost + specs angle
- CEO → Partnership + reliability angle
- Packaging Manager → Technical specs + quality angle
- **Default:** Create 2 Position-based sequences

**If all positions buy the same way:**
- Create 1 universal sequence
- **Note:** This is rare; most B2B sales benefit from position differentiation

---

## OPTIMAL SEQUENCE STRUCTURE (2 Sequences Maximum)

### For Position-Based Clients (Stojkov, Birografika, PRTronic):

**SEQUENCE A: Operational & Technical**
- Targets: Procurement/Purchasing, Packaging/Technical Managers, Supply Chain/Operations
- Messaging angle: Specs, cost, capacity, technical feasibility, reliability
- Email focus: "Here's how we meet your production requirements"

**SEQUENCE B: Strategic & Brand**
- Targets: CEO/Owner/Leadership, Brand/Production Managers, Design/Quality
- Messaging angle: Partnership, reliability, brand fit, positioning, certifications
- Email focus: "Here's why we're the right long-term partner"

---

## KEY RULES (Hard Constraints)

1. **Maximum 2 sequences per client** (unless explicitly approved by client leadership)
2. **Avoid more than 2 sequences unless:**
   - Client explicitly requests industry-specific sequences in their ICP
   - Different industries have fundamentally different buying processes (rare)
3. **ICP Documentation MUST clearly state:**
   - "Sequence structure: Position-based OR Industry-based"
   - "Number of sequences: 1 OR 2"
   - "Sequence A targets: [list positions]"
   - "Sequence B targets: [list positions]"
4. **Consolidate tiers intelligently:**
   - Don't create separate sequences for adjacent tiers
   - Group TIER 1+2+3 together (operational/technical decision-makers)
   - Group TIER 4+5+6 together (strategic/brand influencers and leadership)

## EXAMPLES BY CLIENT

### STOJKOV (Print & Packaging Manufacturer)
- **Structure:** Position-based
- **Rationale:** Serves multiple industries (Cosmetics, Food, Pharma, Publishing, Gifting) but procurement journey is SIMILAR across all industries
- **Sequence A:** Operational & Technical (TIER 1+2+3: Procurement, Packaging Manager, Supply Chain)
- **Sequence B:** Strategic & Brand (TIER 4+5+6: CEO/Owner, Brand Manager, Quality Manager)

### BIROGRAFIKA (Label & Print Manufacturer)
- **Structure:** Position-based
- **Rationale:** Serves niche (labels for FMCG/food/beverage) but procurement process is the same across customer industries
- **Sequence A:** Operational & Technical (TIER 1+2+3: Procurement, Packaging Manager, Supply Chain)
- **Sequence B:** Strategic & Brand (TIER 4+5+6: CEO/Owner, Brand Manager, Quality Manager)

### PRTRONIC (Display Solutions for OEMs)
- **Structure:** Position-based
- **Rationale:** OEM customers have similar buying processes regardless of end-market (industrial, terminal, kiosk)
- **Sequence A:** Technical & Specification (Engineering/Product Leadership: assess display fit for equipment)
- **Sequence B:** Supply & Procurement (Purchasing Manager: review supply reliability and pricing)

## DECISION CHECKLIST

Before creating sequences, confirm:
- [ ] ICP clearly states: Position-based OR Industry-based?
- [ ] Total sequences = 2?
- [ ] Sequence A + B cover all target positions?
- [ ] No position is missing from both sequences?
- [ ] Messaging angle is distinct between A and B?
- [ ] No overlap in email messaging between sequences?
- [ ] All 3 email templates (Initial + 2 Follow-ups) created for each sequence?
