# Seyens - Ideal Customer Profile

## Best-Fit Customers (Real Examples)

- **IBM Research Zurich** - Advanced corporate research environment; repeat customer
- **Roche** - Large science-led organization with substantial researcher populations
- **Boehringer Ingelheim** - Research-intensive pharmaceutical environment with scientists as core audience

**Common denominator:** Substantial scientific/R&D workforce, recurring communication needs, organizational mechanism for staff development

## Dream Customers (Illustrative Profiles)

- **Novartis** - Global pharmaceutical R&D with large scientific teams and multiple sites
- **ASML** - Highly technical, research-intensive engineering organization
- **NVIDIA Research** - Large populations of advanced technical researchers
- **AstraZeneca, Novo Nordisk, Infineon, BioNTech**

*These are examples of the profile, not a closed target list. Goal: uncover comparable companies we don't already know.*

## Bad-Fit Customers (Exclusions)

- Very early-stage startups with fewer than ~20-30 researchers and no structured L&D budget
- Companies looking for generic presentation, sales-pitch or PowerPoint training
- Organizations buying only low-cost, high-volume lectures (cannot support interactive cohort training)
- Teams where the need is primarily marketing/brand design rather than scientists communicating technical work
- Large companies where the contact has no connection to R&D learning or no path to budget owner

## Company Size Criteria

### R&D Population is Key (Total Employee Count is a Rough Proxy)

- **Ideal:** Identifiable R&D population of 100+ scientists/engineers (no upper limit)
- **Good:** 50+ scientists when structured L&D and clear cohort exists
- **For R&D-heavy biotech/deep-tech:** Companies from ~200 total employees can be attractive
- **For diversified industrial companies:** Prioritize 1,000+ employees unless substantial research unit can be identified

**Sweet spot:** Large enough to support repeated cohorts but not so diffuse that no one owns researcher development

## Geographic Focus

### Secondary Markets

- Rest of Europe

*No hard geographic restriction. Prioritize high-income, research-intensive markets where English-language specialist training is normal and Seyens pricing is realistic.*

## Business Model Indicators

### Particularly Strong Fits

- Multi-site or global R&D organizations
- Companies with internal R&D academy, learning curriculum or dedicated L&D support for scientists/engineers
- Organizations with regular research symposia, poster sessions, technical conferences or scientific onboarding
- Research cultures with high publication, patent and conference activity
- Companies where one successful engagement can expand across teams, sites or yearly cohorts

*High growth/VC backing alone is not a sufficient signal unless it translates into meaningful R&D workforce and training budget.*

## Target Industries

### PRIMARY: Life Sciences (Pharmaceuticals, Biotechnology)

Pharma and biotech R&D with large scientific teams and multiple sites

- Complex technical content requiring clear visual communication
- Large PhD/research populations
- Frequent internal/external scientific presentations
- Established L&D budgets for scientist development

### SECONDARY: Industrial & Deep-Tech R&D

AI, semiconductors, photonics, advanced materials, chemicals, advanced manufacturing

- Highly technical, research-intensive engineering organizations
- Complex internal and external technical communication needs
- Large populations of advanced technical researchers

### TERTIARY: Medtech, Diagnostics & Scientific Instruments

Science-led technology companies with research-intensive cultures

- High publication, patent and conference activity
- Meaningful share of staff conducting original research
