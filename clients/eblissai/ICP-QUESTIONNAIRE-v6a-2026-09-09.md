## 1. Your Company & Customers

### 1.1 What do you sell and who is it for?

eBliss is an **Autonomous Execution Layer** that diagnoses and resolves endpoint IT issues and performs novel, complex work directly using AI, reasoning through each problem and activity firsthand rather than matching it against a pre-built fix.

It's for Digital Workplace/EUC and IT Ops teams at **mid and large enterprises** who need to close the gap between tools that flag problems and issues that are actually fixed - without adding headcount, while dramatically decreasing cost and preventing productivity and revenue loss.

### 1.2 Best Customers (ICP)

- **Core ICP:** Organization with **3,000 or more endpoints** struggling to close L2 and L3 tickets
- Currently engaging with multiple enterprise customers and GSI partners in parallel evaluations (just came out of stealth)
- Example: SVP of End User Computing at Fortune 250 financial services company (10,000+ endpoints) - pain point was tools that flag issues but don't provide "an easy button to go fix it"
- **Common feedback:** Existing DEX/telemetry tools surface symptoms on dashboards; humans still do diagnosis and fix manually (raised across Deloitte, EY, LTM, TechMahindra, Compass Group calls)

## 2. Why Now & Pain Points

### 2.1 Trigger Events (What happens before they buy?)

- Failed workaround or growing backlog of unresolved Level 2+ tickets that existing DEX/EUM/ITSM tools can flag but not fix
- **Growing human demands on IT management work** (NEW)
- New IT Ops/Digital Workplace leader under pressure to cut MTTR (mean time to repair) **and prevent productivity and revenue loss** (NEW)
- L2/L3 **and IT Management Operations** need to be streamlined and made more efficient

### 2.2 Problems Customer Experiences

- **High MTTR** driven by tickets sitting in queue requiring manual, repetitive investigation across Windows/Mac/Linux endpoints - admins can see something's wrong but have no fast path to fixing it
- **High unscheduled downtime of devices leading to productivity loss** (NEW)
- **Critical application failure for extended periods leading to revenue loss** (NEW)
- **Attrition / Loss of tribal knowledge** - eBliss captures and retains operational knowledge within the system itself

### 2.3 Business Impact

#### Tier 1 — Direct Financial Exposure:
- Cost Exposure - direct dollar cost of open/unresolved backlog
- Support Team Time Lost - L1/L2/L3 man-hours + L3 capacity reclaimed

#### Tier 2 — Operational Impact:
- End-User Productivity Loss - blocked employee hours × loaded cost
- SLA Breaches - contractual/reputational risk from missed resolution commitments
- **Revenue loss - Critical application crashes / failures** (NEW)

#### Tier 3 — Strategic/Organizational Risk (narrative only):
- Talent & Retention - attrition risk from senior staff stuck on repetitive work
- Security Incident Response - MTTR-adjacent risk on security-flagged tickets
- Compliance & Audit Readiness - most acute in regulated industries
- Strategic Capacity & Scale - Run/Grow/Transform budget shift + ability to absorb growth without proportional headcount growth

### 2.4 Urgency Drivers (Why solve it NOW?)

#### Triggering Events (highest urgency):
- Recent major outage or high-profile incident (Southwest/CrowdStrike-style) that made status quo board-level topic
- String of SLA breaches costing real money or specific client relationship
- Security incident where slow triage/response was identified as root-cause contributor

#### Structural Pressure (building urgency):
- Headcount freeze or hiring freeze + growing endpoint count/ticket volume - math stops working
- Recent wave of senior L2/L3 attrition from burnout on repetitive work
- New CIO/VP within last 6-12 months - actively looking for visible win

#### Calendar-Driven Urgency:
- Budget cycle timing - Q4 planning for next fiscal year, or unspent budget
- Upcoming compliance audit or regulatory exam
- DEX or ITSM contract renewal coming up

#### Competitive/Peer Pressure:
- Direct competitor or peer company publicly announced AI-driven IT ops investment

**Note:** Most accounts won't have clean trigger event - this is why calculator matters (makes Tier 1 cost number visible and specific to their numbers)

## 3. Why You (Differentiation)

### 3.1 Why customers choose eBliss

Existing DEX/EUM/ITSM tools (Nexthink, Lakeside SysTrack, etc.) tell IT something's wrong; **eBliss figures out what's wrong and fixes it**, with little or no human intervention.

Over doing nothing or building in-house: eBliss ships with proprietary Knowledge Graph and guardrails so autonomous execution is safe and accurate from day one, and lets the same admin team support meaningfully more end uses without adding headcount - the same leverage shift tools like GitHub Copilot and Cursor drove for software engineers.

### 3.2 Customer Testimonials

**SVP of End User Computing, Fortune 250 company:**
> "A lot of these tools can tell us what's wrong, but they're not giving us that easy button to go fix it. I'm really looking for how to fix, and then quickly turning that into a script that I could deploy across the environment."

**Senior Technical Lead, global professional services company:**

## APPENDIX: Detailed Buyer Persona Frameworks

### Persona 1: Digital Workplace / EUC — Fortune 500
**Buying Center Targeting Priority:** PRIMARY

**Framing:** Default entry point for most eBliss deals. The leader (EVP/SVP/VP/Director/Head of Digital Workplace or EUC) owns the end-user device fleet and support experience. Unlike I&O, measured on their own slice of the estate specifically — endpoint cost, endpoint support quality, endpoint compliance — not aggregate infrastructure. This is also the persona every other buying center (I&O, CTO/CAIO) ultimately routes down to for actual pilot execution.

**Priorities (what this role is measured on):**

1. **Priority 1: Cost per endpoint / cost per ticket**
   - Usually #1 metric an EUC leader is measured on
   - KPI: cost per managed device, cost per resolved ticket, headcount ratio (devices per tech)
   - **eBliss fit:** Strong — autonomous L2 resolution directly attacks cost-per-ticket by resolving issues without human tech touching them. Best opening wedge with this persona

2. **Priority 2: End-user experience / Digital Employee Experience (DEX)**
   - Increasingly #2 metric as EUC leaders face pressure to prove IT support isn't productivity drag
   - KPI: mean time to resolution (MTTR), first-contact resolution rate, employee CSAT/NPS on IT support, "digital friction" scores
   - **eBliss fit:** Strong — faster autonomous resolution directly improves MTTR and first-contact resolution, less downtime/frustration translates to better employee sentiment scores

3. **Priority 3: Patch/vulnerability compliance (Patch Tuesday, CVE response time)**
   - Real, recurring pain at any regulated Fortune 500
   - KPI: % devices patched within SLA window, mean time to patch critical CVEs, audit findings tied to patch compliance
   - **eBliss fit:** Moderate-to-strong — strongest when eBliss's remediation extends into patch deployment and verification, not just detection. This is concrete "issue identified → fix deployed" gap most EUC leaders describe as biggest frustration

4. **Priority 4: Large-scale transformation initiatives (AVD/Windows 365, physical-to-virtual migration, OS/hardware refresh cycles)**
   - Multi-year, budget-line projects with hard deadlines
   - KPI: migration completion %, on-time/on-budget delivery, post-migration incident volume
   - **eBliss fit:** Strong when applicable — autonomous remediation absorbs ticket-volume spike during cutover without requiring surge headcount. Conditional applicability (not every account has one active), surface via discovery qualifying question

**Messaging/Approach Tips:**
- Lead with Priority 1 (cost per ticket) as opener — broadest, safest, number this leader reports upward most consistently
- Priority 3 (Patch/CVE) is strongest differentiated claim, not opener — directly answers "tools tell me what's wrong but not how to fix it" pain point. Use once you have attention, as proof eBliss closes the loop
- Priority 2 (DEX) works well as second-touch angle or supporting color inside cost-per-ticket pitch — emotional/political layer on top of financial case
- Priority 4 (migration) is conditional — don't lead with it or assume it applies. Surface via existing qualifying question once in conversation
- **Suggested 3-touch cadence:** Touch 1 = cost per ticket. Touch 2 = Patch Tuesday/CVE closing-the-loop story. Touch 3 = DEX/employee experience as reinforcing proof, paired with specific ask (pilot conversation)

**Gate (pass/fail requirement):**
- Security review: Any tool taking autonomous action on endpoints draws scrutiny — audit logging, approval workflows, blast radius, rollback capability all examined
- **eBliss fit:** Moderate-strong — same underlying audit-trail strength as I&O's Priority 2, but here it's pass/fail gate to clear rather than headline pitch angle

**Friction Points:**
- CISO/Security — will push back on autonomous action; expect deep questions on audit logging, approval workflows, rollback. Usually longest pole in tent at regulated account
- Network/Infrastructure — territorial about anything touching endpoint connectivity; same friction pattern seen with LTM RDP issue
- Service Desk/Helpdesk leadership (where distinct from EUC) — can feel threatened if eBliss framed as "replacing" L2 techs rather than freeing them for higher-value work
- Finance/Procurement — cost-per-ticket savings must be provable with hard before/after pilot numbers, not vendor case studies
- App owners — any remediation touching app-specific configs needs their sign-off, slows "autonomous" claims

### Persona 2: EVP/SVP of Infrastructure & Operations (I&O) — Fortune 500
**Buying Center Targeting Priority:** SECONDARY

**Framing:** Owns full IT operating budget and entire ticket funnel — infrastructure, network, EUC, Service Desk all roll up to them. Personally evaluated on aggregate performance across whole estate, not any single team's numbers. Reports results up to CIO and often the board.

**Priorities:**

1. **Priority 1: Cost per managed asset / headcount-scaling ratio**
   - Aggregate version of #1 EUC metric, scaled up
   - KPI: IT opex as % of revenue, cost per managed asset (blended server + endpoint), device/user growth vs. headcount trend
   - **eBliss fit:** Strong, but must be framed in aggregate terms — position eBliss's endpoint savings as contribution to total number. Lean on "scale without linear headcount growth" angle as forward-looking companion to backward-looking cost-savings story

2. **Priority 2: Audit/compliance findings (ITGC/PCI-DSS)**
   - Personally on hook for audit outcomes at regulated Fortune 500
   - KPI: number of audit findings, time-to-remediate findings, clean audit cycles
   - **eBliss fit:** Strong — because eBliss executes remediation via logged, authorized action on endpoint, every fix generates timestamped audit trail as byproduct of doing work. Concrete, provable evidence to show auditors with less manual effort

3. **Priority 3: Aggregate incident/ticket volume and MTTR**
   - Owns full ticket funnel, not just one team's slice
   - KPI: total ticket volume, blended MTTR, first-contact resolution across all tiers
   - **eBliss fit:** Strong — autonomous resolution shortens funnel itself: tickets that would wait for L2 capacity get resolved immediately, compressing MTTR, and Service Desk triage volume drops since fewer tickets need escalation

4. **Priority 4: Uptime/reliability across the estate**
   - SLA/SLO adherence spanning network, infra, endpoints combined
   - KPI: uptime %, SLA breach count, aggregate MTTR
   - **eBliss fit:** Moderate-strong — faster remediation reduces duration of endpoint-originated incidents. Directly reduces exposure on incident-duration component of SLA risk, though still share exposure with network/infra teams for non-endpoint causes

**Messaging/Approach Tips:**
- Lead with Priority 1 (Cost per managed asset/headcount-scaling ratio). Safest, broadest opener — applies regardless of whether account is regulated
- Reserve Priority 2 (Audit/compliance) as strongest follow-up, not opener — sharper and more provable, but only lands if account known to be regulated. Hold for touch two or three if vertical/regulatory status unconfirmed
- Use aggregate language, not team-level language. This persona thinks in totals across whole estate — say "total IT operating cost" or "cost per managed asset," not "ticket resolution cost" or "L2 efficiency"
- Don't lead with uptime/reliability (Priority 4). It's shared-credit metric — this persona knows chunk of uptime risk sits with network/infra teams
- Don't mention ticket-volume calculator or any tool/asset that isn't fully built and confirmed live
- **Suggested 3-touch cadence:** Touch 1 = cost/headcount-scaling. Touch 2 = audit/compliance (if regulated) or ticket volume/MTTR (if not). Touch 3 = uptime/reliability as reinforcing proof, paired with specific ask

**Gate:**
- Security posture (fleet-wide): Vulnerability/patch compliance across servers and endpoints. Not headline KPI at this level, but bar every new tool must clear
- **eBliss fit:** Moderate-strong on endpoint slice — treat as required gate to clear early in sales process, not differentiator to lead with

**Friction Points:**
- CISO — wider blast radius than EUC (servers, not just endpoints), heavier review cycle
- CTO/Platform Engineering — peer branch under CIO; territorial friction over infra-adjacent tooling
- Network Operations — same connectivity friction pattern as LTM RDP issue
- Finance/Procurement — competes directly against cloud modernization budget line; needs sharper, faster ROI proof
- Digital Workplace/EUC (subordinate) — adoption risk if tool feels imposed from above rather than chosen

### Persona 3: CTO / Chief AI Officer (CAIO) / Chief Architect — Technology Strategy & Architecture
**Buying Center Targeting Priority:** SECONDARY

**Framing:** This CTO reports to CIO and owns Platform Engineering and Cloud Engineering & Ops — their day-to-day organization doesn't touch eBliss's endpoint scope at all. Their relevance to eBliss is entirely as sponsor of top-down AI/transformation mandate, not as operational evaluator. **Engage only when there's confirmed signal of active mandate** — this is NOT a persona to run scaled, always-on outbound sequence against the way EUC or I&O are.

**Priorities:**

1. **Priority 1: Board/CEO-visible AI transformation progress**
   - Core mandate: demonstrate enterprise is meaningfully adopting AI, not just discussing it
   - KPI: # of AI use cases in production (not pilot), enterprise-wide adoption metrics, board/investor narrative progress
   - **eBliss fit:** Strong as proof point, not headline — live, production autonomous-remediation deployment is concrete "AI actually working, not just a slide" example this exec can point to

2. **Priority 2: Speed/breadth of AI pilot-to-production conversion**
   - Known failure mode: many AI pilots launch, few reach production
   - KPI: pilot-to-production conversion rate, time-to-scale after successful pilot
   - **eBliss fit:** Strong — clean, fast pilot-to-production path is exactly "this one actually worked" story that helps this exec's own scorecard

3. **Priority 3: Responsible AI governance/risk framework maturity**
   - As AI adoption scales, this role increasingly owns (or co-owns with CISO/Legal) governance framework
   - KPI: governance framework completeness, audit/compliance sign-off rate on AI initiatives, incident-free track record for deployed AI
   - **eBliss fit:** Strong — same underlying mechanism as I&O's audit-trail fit: eBliss's authorized, logged, timestamped action trail is governance asset this exec can point to as "how we do AI safely"

**Messaging/Approach Tips:**
- Don't cold-outreach this persona on fixed cadence the way EUC/I&O get worked — only engage once there's specific, confirmed signal of active AI mandate (CAIO hire announcement, public AI-strategy statement, press coverage of transformation initiative)
- Lead with Priority 2 (pilot-to-production speed) when reaching out — most concrete, provable claim and avoids sounding like generic "AI is important" pitch
- Use Priority 1 (board-visible progress) as framing/context for why conversation matters to them personally, not as opening claim itself — it's "why you should care" backdrop, not proof point
- Hold Priority 3 (governance) for second touch or actual conversation — leading with governance/compliance can read as friction rather than value on cold first touch
- Never position eBliss as THE AI transformation story — position it as one concrete, working proof point inside their larger narrative
- Every message should make clear actual pilot execution happens with Digital Workplace/EUC — this exec is sponsor/opener, not implementer

**Gate:**
- Security/data-governance review: Any AI tool touching production systems will need CISO and/or Legal sign-off before this exec attaches their name as sponsor — their personal credibility is on line
- **eBliss fit:** Moderate — same underlying strength as audit-trail story in Priority 3, but this is pass/fail gate to clear, not pitch angle

**Friction Points:**
- CIO — if CIO is more business-facing exec, there can be tension over who "owns" AI narrative; engaging this CTO/CAIO without CIO awareness risks political misstep
- CISO/Legal — AI governance is jointly owned territory; expect this exec to defer risk/compliance sign-off rather than decide unilaterally
- Digital Workplace/EUC (operational team) — mandate-from-above friction: deal sponsored top-down can meet resistance from team that has to actually run it if they feel it was imposed rather than chosen
- Finance/Board — this exec spends political capital on AI ROI broadly; if earlier AI investments haven't shown returns, may be real skepticism to overcome

### Persona 4: Service Desk / Incident Management — Fortune 500
**Buying Center Targeting Priority:** TERTIARY

**Framing:** Tertiary, conditional buying center — only applies when L2 resolution work is absorbed into Service Desk rather than sitting directly under Digital Workplace/EUC (qualifying question determines this). When applicable, this leader owns ticket intake, triage, escalation, and — since Major Incident Paging is subordinate to Incident Management — major-incident response process. Unlike EUC, which owns the fix itself, this role owns the funnel the ticket travels through before (and sometimes instead of) reaching technical resolver.

**Priorities:**

1. **Priority 1: Ticket throughput and SLA adherence**
   - Core operational metric for this role — how many tickets move through funnel, how fast, whether they clear SLA windows
   - KPI: tickets resolved per tech, average queue wait time, SLA compliance %
   - **eBliss fit:** Strong — autonomous resolution removes tickets from funnel before they consume queue time or tech capacity, directly improving throughput and SLA compliance without adding headcount

2. **Priority 2: First-contact resolution / escalation rate**
   - Measured on how much gets resolved at first contact versus escalated further up chain
   - KPI: first-contact resolution rate (FCR), escalation rate to L2/L3
   - **eBliss fit:** Strong — autonomous resolution effectively raises "first-contact" bar, since tickets that would have required escalation get resolved automatically instead, improving this leader's own FCR number

3. **Priority 3: Major incident response time**
   - Since Major Incident Paging sits under this role, personally on hook for how fast major incident gets detected, paged, resolved
   - KPI: time-to-detect, time-to-page, MTTR for major (P1/P2) incidents
   - **eBliss fit:** Moderate — eBliss can speed resolution once endpoint-related issue identified, but major incident response typically involves broader coordination (network, infra, comms) that eBliss doesn't directly manage. Position as contributing factor on endpoint-cause subset, not whole story

4. **Priority 4: Staffing/scheduling efficiency**
   - Often measured on agent utilization and whether staffing scales efficiently with ticket volume
   - KPI: agent utilization rate, tickets per agent per shift, overtime/overflow staffing costs
   - **eBliss fit:** Strong — same mechanism as elsewhere: autonomous resolution absorbs ticket volume without requiring additional shift coverage

**Messaging/Approach Tips:**
- Before treating account as Service Desk target at all, confirm relevance via existing qualifying question ("Is your L2 team part of Service Desk, or is that work absorbed into Digital Workplace/EUC team directly?") — if L2 sits with Digital Workplace, this persona isn't right target
- Lead with Priority 1 (throughput/SLA) — broadest, most universally reported metric for this role
- Priority 2 (FCR/escalation rate) is strong, natural second-touch angle — personal to how this leader's own performance gets judged, distinct from funnel-level Priority 1 number
- Don't lead with Priority 3 (major incident response) — it's real but narrower in scope, and eBliss's fit there is partial
- Priority 4 (staffing efficiency) works well paired with Priority 1 in same message — they reinforce same underlying "do more without adding headcount" story
- **Suggested 3-touch cadence:** Touch 1 = throughput/SLA. Touch 2 = first-contact resolution rate. Touch 3 = staffing efficiency as reinforcing proof, paired with specific ask

**Gate:**
- ITSM platform integration: Any new tool needs to work within existing ticketing/ITSM system (commonly ServiceNow) rather than operate as parallel, disconnected process
- **eBliss fit:** Moderate — dependent on confirmed integration capability with account's specific ITSM platform; treat as required gate to clear early, not differentiator to lead with

**Friction Points:**
- Digital Workplace/EUC — potential territorial tension in opposite direction from I&O's: if Service Desk champions tool that touches endpoint remediation, EUC (if organizationally distinct) may feel it's being bypassed on its own turf
- CISO/Security — same autonomous-action scrutiny as other personas; audit logging and approval workflows will be examined
- ITSM platform/tooling team — owns ServiceNow (or equivalent) configuration and may need to be involved in any integration work
- I&O (parent org) — since Service Desk reports up through I&O, any significant tool decision may need at least tacit sign-off from that level
